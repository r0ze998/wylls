## 0. The contract in one page

1. **What we build.** **Labelled** AI citizens, equal per nation (W5): **12** (2 per nation, deck-2) in the A/B and the live recording, **18** (3 per nation, deck-3, O-AI-2) in the main run, 6 (deck-1) in the smoke; they play the M1 game with the same keys, relay, herald reads, 30/h bucket and relay quota as humans and the ~180 script bots, on a **local test chain only** (Mode-A `frontier-stack`, test-key drand). Each AI has a persona (temperament, creed, **pact-free goals**), a code-held ledger, a **memory** and a visible social life: signed messages and a nation council that picks one of three code-made targets (the **Strike Order**, code name "Call", §0.4). Memory is code-templated **episodes** derived from public records, retrieved into every prompt, cited by the model in its answer (`mem`), shown on the Wyll card and next to every released decision (§5). Pacts and betrayal are **[DEFERRED]** (Appendix A).
2. **How a decision is made (W7).** The bot process (`frontier-bots --brain`, AI slots only) observes as today, sends its duties at once, turns economy and military choices into **≤ 12 code-made candidates** (each with facts and entities), and asks the keyless **mind** (`frontier-citizens`, Node) for a choice. The mind gates the call (wake events incl. a pulse, budgets, deadline), attaches memory references to the candidates, renders a ≤ 3k-token prompt, calls local **Gemma 4 26B A4B** (llama.cpp on 41901, thinking off, T = 0, JSON-schema-constrained), validates (V0–V3, V5), and answers with candidate ids, the episode handles the decision used (`mem`), **standing orders** and caps. The bot re-observes if the answer is stale, re-checks (V6), signs and sends. **Anything late, invalid or refused runs the rule autopilot, filtered:** the autopilot keeps economy and duties and the Strike-Order follow, and **never starts a voluntary march** (§3.6). Therefore every voluntary AI march is `by:model` or a council Strike Order.
3. **What is published.** The AI's own static server (`citizens/serve.mjs`, 41902, same origin as the council page) serves `PUB` as `/h/ai/*` and forwards `/h/*` to the herald and `/f/ai/*` to the social service: the signed roster (AI, script-bot and seat labels), pre-genesis commitments, Wyll cards **with their memory block**, per-AI episode files, per-bell message files (ballots as leaves only), council files, a minimal chronicle, decision records (march decisions as commitments only **until their release**, then opened with candidates, cited memory and reason) and per-bell roots. A keyed **registrar** anchors each bell's roots with a Memo on the local chain. `verify-minds` checks commitments, roster deal, roots and openings, tx↔record coverage, speech provenance, an episode replay and a 20-decision bit replay (M1, M2, M3, M7, M8, M9, M11).
4. **What we show.** Recorded from the main run, council page only, labelled "local test chain", in this order: the roster with labels; one AI's Wyll card (goals, relationships, memory lines); **an AI citizen's own march decision** (candidate list, its "Remembered" lines, its own words, `by: model`), its army leaving (destination sealed), and, once the arrival bell has ended and the march is revealed (§7.2), the opened reason, the destination and the **real clash report**; then the council beat (an AI moves an option, the presenter's seat ballots, the Strike Order stays sealed, the strike result); then the A/B table, `verify-minds` and the memory probe. If no model-chosen march produced a clash (G12, Y = 0), the council beat is the headline and the pitch says so.
5. **Hard limits.** No program, ABI, kernel, MC or **herald** file change (§0.2). No devnet. No paid API, no Anthropic or OpenAI key in any process, `advisor.mjs` stays off (W1), every service talks to loopback only. Keys never reach the mind or the model (a structural property, not secrecy: all keys here are public-derivable local test keys, §12.1 R9). The design session's files are never edited (§11.1). AI is always labelled (EU AI Act Art. 50; Anthropic usage policy). D9 (first holding never taken) and every M1 test stay untouched and green. No capture exists in this build and none is claimed (§0.5).
6. **Delivery (from 2026-10-04).** 12 units (AC1a, AC1b, AC2, AC3a, AC3b, AC4, AC5, AC6a, AC6, AC7, AC8, AC9) in two build waves with a **slice gate on 10-05**, an integration-fix unit AC10, and a run wave (§11): W-A 10-04→10-06, I-A 10-06/07, W-B 10-07→10-08, I-B 10-08/09, then **A/B first** (10-09 afternoon), main run overnight at 10× (10-09/10), recording 10-11, **I-C merge and packaging into the submission branch (10-12 morning)**, freeze 10-12 23:59 JST, submission by 10-13 15:59 JST. The order of work follows the dependency chain (memory and the own march before the council), not the calendar; §11.8 gives the time-cut order.

## 0.3 The owner's words that govern v1.2 (translated from Japanese)

- 2026-10-03: the game is a Civ-like, MMO-like civilization simulation in a shared world: land, choose a nation, build, expand the army, gather food, march, wage war, one action at a time while the whole game's time flows. AI agents are the heart of the game; the hidden deterministic Shades are not AI; the AI NPCs must work as AI players no different from human players; Eternum's Daydreams is a large reference and whether it can be implemented is the core. "Wylls" means will: how does a game behave when AI has will like humans, and this must be implemented reliably. The owner asked to be evaluated flatly, not trusted blindly.
- 2026-10-04: AI-citizen **memory is the core of the product and must be in the hackathon build**. Pacts and betrayal between AIs are **not** included (memory has priority). Design first; if there are no conflicts, proceed; do not adjust the plan by schedule: adopt the best form for game development.
- Where an item below and these words disagree, these words win. Nothing here claims the model "wants" or "intends"; "will" is **built by the system and observable** (§5.9).

## 0.5 What a battle is in this build (facts the prompt and the claims rest on)

[source: `permutation-rules/src/frontier/{camp,holding,travel}.rs`, `agents/src/policy.rs`]

- **Camp:** always a legal target (shields do not apply); 100–400 troops; none in rings 0–1, one per province in rings ≥ 2 at the start, then respawn with chance ½ per game day only in provinces that hold a village (camp.rs `place`); finite, and about 180 script bots contend for them. Clearing one gives **10 Works** (points with no use yet).
- **Enemy open-field stack** (another nation's army on a non-site tile): legal at any time.
- **Enemy village:** legal only when the target's shield has ended at the arrival bell **and** the sender's own village shield is over (`policy::shield_refuses` checks both). The shield is 48 game hours (`SHIELD_SECS`), i.e. **288 bells after founding**, about 4.8 real hours at 10×. A 2-game-day run ends at the moment villages first become attackable.
- **Beating another nation yields no loot, no capture and no Works** (D9, I-17); no village can be taken; the map does not change in M1. A march arrives at least 2 bells after departure; for a 12-hex march the first clash report appears about 31–41 game minutes after the march leaves [model: DESIGN-OVERVIEW §3.1, on `frontier/unify`], i.e. **3–4 real minutes at 10×**; a farther camp (up to 32 hexes and 4 provinces, 2 game minutes a flat hex) takes an hour of game time or more (about 6–7 real minutes at 10×). "Next bell" is never claimed.
- A host marches rarely: every Depart charges 74 of the 120-point stamina cap [source: `travel::march_stamina(MAX_PATH_STEPS)` = 10 + 2 × 32, used by `policy::can_depart` (`policy.rs` 443–453); `host::STAMINA_CAP` = 120; there is no `DEPART_STAMINA` constant], stamina regenerates +1 per bell and an engagement costs 20 (`host::ENGAGE_STAMINA`), so a full host can depart again after about 28 bells (4.7 game hours) and, after a march that also fought, after about 48 bells (8 game hours) [estimate; AC3a step 0 (d) measures it]; there is no recall of an in-flight march (a "recall" is a new Depart of an arrived army); there is no merge or split instruction, no supply attrition and no enforced Works cap in the program. The prompt and V5 never mention them.
- The only legitimate reasons to attack another nation in M1 are **memory- and persona-based** (an answered grievance, a removed threat, goal progress, a Strike Order): candidates carry no fabricated reward (§4.3), and the prompt says: "No land can be captured this season. Battles cost troops only. Camps give 10 Works, points with no use yet."

## 2. Personas and the Wyll card

### 2.1 Persona model

A persona is `{id, ambition, temperament, creeds[6]{en,ja}, goals[4]}`. Temperament is 7 integers in 0..100: `aggression, loyalty, ambition, honesty, risk, sociability, grudge`. The prompt shows each as one of five words (0–19 very low, 20–39 low, 40–59 moderate, 60–79 high, 80–100 very high). Temperament changes **only** the prompt and two code rules:

1. **Trust decay** (§5.1): once per game day each trust part moves toward 0 by `d = ceil((100 − grudge) / 20)`; toward citizens of the AI's own nation the step is `max(1, d − floor(loyalty / 34))` (loyalty is slower decay of trust inside the nation; it is **not** about keeping promises). Test vectors in `citizens-persona.test.mjs`.
2. `messages_cap = 6 + round(sociability / 25)`, capped at 10 per game day.

`honesty` is a prompt word only. No creed, goal or temperament rule mentions pacts, promises, alliances, keeping a word or betrayal (they are deferred, App. A).

## 3. The decision loop

### 3.1 Cadence and step order (pinned)

Brain bots step **every bell**, at **90–150 game-s into the bell**: the eager offset, because at 0–20 s nearly every resident action is `NotResident` (the province has not usually resolved bell − 2; W4-F) [source: `fleet.rs` `Pace::plan/after`, `eager` branch]. `eager` applies only when `spec.persona.is_some()` and an AI bot has none, so the `fleet.rs` hook sets the eager cadence and offset for brain bots. At 10× a bell is 60 s real: the step starts 9–15 s into the bell and a session's real deadline is about 45 s minus the margin (§3.5). **`/v1/decide` is idempotent per `(index, bell)`:** when a step nudges, `fleet.rs` re-runs the bot 45–75 game-s later (up to `NUDGE_RETRIES`); a same-bell repeat returns the cached answer (no model call, **no new record**) and the brain re-observes and sends from it, so "one wake per AI per bell" holds, and a step that reaches the mind leaves exactly one record (v1.3, R5: a step before the bot has a home holding or a session makes no mind call and leaves no record; the brain counts it `no_session`, §7.2). One step, in this order:

1. observe (as today) → `A = policy::decide(&obs,&cx)`, plus the Call-follow intent from `follow.rs` if this AI's host is invited and the Call is not declined (§6.6); read the AI's own **hosts, transits and seals** from `/h/me` (`ai/meview.rs`, §1.3 C2) into its marchbook.
2. **Send duties D ⊆ A at once** (Join, FileTicket, Reveal, SettleTransit, SettleExplore, SettleOwnTicket, Nudge). **Harvest is not a duty (v1.3, R4):** it is an economy action and pauses with Build, Train, Muster and Explore at the quota floor (§3.4), at every AI step.
3. Build the candidates (§4.3) from this observation, then `POST /v1/decide` (§4.1). A gate-closed answer is synchronous (target < 50 ms, no LLM): `mode:"autopilot"` with standing orders.
4. Apply the answer's standing orders and the **autopilot filter** to A's economy/military part (§3.6) → `A'`.
5. **Staleness:** if the answer is `mode:"model"`, or came more than 30 game-s after step start, re-observe and recompute `A` (and `A'`) before V6 and sending. `plan_march` fixes departure at `obs.now + DEPART_SLACK_SECS` (60 s, policy.rs:47 [source]), so a plan older than 30 game-s is never sent. 30 game-s is 3 s real at 10×, so **every model answer is re-observed**.
6. V6 (§4.5) on the chosen intents against the fresh obs; send; `POST /v1/outcome` (with the marchbook entries the mind needs for memory, §4.1 `own_marches`).

**Herald load (v1.3, R6).** Besides the observation of today, the brain makes extra herald GETs: the Recall threat feed (`/h/events`), up to 3 owner-province GETs per step, and `obs_digest` re-reads of `/h/me` and every observed province, plus the path fetch of §4.3. They are accepted for the hackathon and **not capped** (v1.2 said "≤ 8 extra GETs per step"): they are measured, and the run report prints **GETs per step per AI** and the herald load during the run (§10.1).

Test `ai_brain_stale.rs`: a fake mind that sleeps past 30 game-s ⇒ the brain observes twice and sends intents planned from the second obs.

### 3.2 Wake events (public facts only)

One deduplicated wake per AI per bell: all events of b are merged into one gate decision.

| Code | Event | Source | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|

| W-CLASH | a **real** clash: a CLASH record with an arrival or an engagement at a province where the AI holds a village or has a host, or that lists one of its hosts (this includes the AI's **own** marches arriving); a roll-call CLASH record (resident hosts, no arrival, no engagement) does not wake (v1.3, R3) | feed (wave A) | 4 |
| W-THREAT | a DEPART of another nation's host whose origin is ≤ 3 provinces from the AI's home (origin, arrive_bell, dep_mass; destination sealed) | feed (wave A) | 2 (+1 if dep_mass ≥ ½ of home troops) |

**W-CLASH mode (v1.3, R3).** The default is `clashMode: "real"` (real clashes only). The literal reading of v1.2 (a wake on every CLASH record at such a province) is kept as the test option `clashMode: "literal"` of AC6a's feed. Measured by AC6a on its capture (12 stand-in citizens, the first two to join each nation, 7.2 game days, 1,033 bells): **literal 3,449 W-CLASH wakes** (up to 684 of 1,033 bells for one citizen), **default 206** (3 to 35 per citizen, about 0.4 to 5 per game day); 20,857 of the 21,674 captured CLASH records (96 %) have no engagement. With weight 4 (≥ the gate's 3) the literal reading would open a session on most bells, which the budgets of §3.3 are not designed for.

**Social cap:** W-DM + W-HALL together contribute at most 2 to `s`, so social traffic alone never opens a session (only reactions). Nothing else wakes a mind; in particular no keeper, relay or journal data. (v1.1's W-PACT, weight 4, is **[DEFERRED]**, App. A.)

### 3.3 Delta gate (exact)

Let `s` = the capped sum of b's weights, `left` = the remaining budget (§3.4).

1. `bell ≥ end_bell − 2` → autopilot (`"season_end"`).
2. No roster yet, or the AI has no final holding → autopilot (`"not_ready"`).
3. **Session** if `s ≥ 3` and `left.sessions > 0` and (`b − last ≥ 2` or `s ≥ 6`).
4. Else **reaction** if any of W-DM, W-HALL, W-CALL is present, `left.reactions > 0`, and the global reaction cap of this bell is not reached (≤ 4 reactions per bell across all AIs).
5. Else autopilot (`"below_gate"` or `"budget"`).
6. Independently: the reflection (W-REFLECT) and the council calls (W-COUNCIL) run as their own jobs; they never consume session or reaction budget.

**Expected volume** [estimate]: per AI per game day ≈ 6 pulse-driven sessions (at most 8 sessions in all, event-driven ones included) + 2 reflections + 2 council calls × 3 periods (main config) ≈ 14–17 model decisions; 12 AIs ≈ 170–200/day (the reflections add ≈ 24 calls a day), 18 AIs ≈ 250–300. At 10× (a game day = 2.4 h) that is ≈ 70–85 calls per real hour against ≈ 400–450/h capacity, i.e. about a fifth (§3.5). A council window opening queues up to 12 (A/B) or 18 (main run) motion calls at once, about 7 s each: that is more than one 60 s bell, and it fits only because the motion deadline is three bells away (`bell_start(C0+3) − margin`).

`margin = max(3 s real, 60 game-s ÷ scale)`. Real times come from the brain's `GameClock` (chain time and `--scale`), sent as `deadline_unix_ms`. Messages ≤ `messages_cap` per game day; model-chosen marches ≤ 4 per game day and the cumulative troop cap (§4.5 V3). On chain the AI has exactly the human limits (30/h bucket, burst 60; relay quota 40/day on days 0–6, then 20; `QUOTA_RESERVE = 6`, which protects only Depart, SettleTransit and Join [source: `bot.rs` 476–490]). **Quota floor (AC3a):** because AI bots step every bell, the autopilot's economy actions (**Harvest, Build, Train, Muster, Explore**: Harvest included, v1.3, R4) pause while `quota_left ≤ 16`, so that model-chosen actions are not starved (model-chosen build, train and muster candidates are sponsored too); candidate facts show `quota`, and the report lists quota left at every model decision and counts quota-starved bells.

### 3.5 Scheduler and the deadline vs the bell

- One llama slot (§7.1). One queue, **earliest deadline first**; ties by kind (session, motion, ballot, reaction, reflection), then AI index. **Reactions never pre-empt a session** whose deadline is within 2 × p90(session).
- Admission: a job goes to autopilot (`"no_time"`) if `now + queue_wait + p90(kind) > deadline`; p90 is the rolling p90 of the last 50 calls of that kind, **seeded from AC1a's probe** (§11.3 AC1a step 0; recorded in `AC1a-NOTES.md`). Planning figure [estimate]: `cache_prompt:false`, ≈ 1.2k system + ≤ 3k variable tokens ⇒ TTFT ≈ 5 s (8–10 s at 3.5k tokens under MC-load speeds), so ≈ 400–450 decisions/h. Spike figures [measured]: TTFT 2.3 s at 2k tokens and 45 s at 24k, generation 70–100 tok/s, 25–32 tok/s under MC load (`gemma4/REPORT.md`).
- The llama request is aborted at the deadline (`"timeout"`). **One retry** on a V0–V2 failure only if `deadline − now ≥ 1.2 × p50(kind)`; the retry appends the validator's one-line reason.
- The brain's HTTP timeout is `deadline − now` (≥ 500 ms, else it does not call and records `"no_time"` locally). A decision landing after `bell_start(b+1)` is **never** executed (`"late"`).
- Every live run (smokes, A/B, main) runs at **10×** (a bell = 60 s real, margin 6 s). With the step offset of §3.1 a session has about 39 s from step start to its deadline (60 − 9…15 s offset − 6 s margin); admission, the p90 seeds and the retry rule use that figure.
- **Step-0 measurements (AC1a, before the budget is frozen):** (a) json_schema candidate-id selection validity and latency on a 3.5k-token prompt (no `json_schema` use exists in the lab [source]); (b) reflection quality and latency on a 2k-token prompt; (c) if p90 > 20 s, reduce the MEMORY block to 600 tokens, **never** the candidate list.

### 3.6 Autopilot, fallback and standing orders

- **Autopilot = the unchanged rule policy** (`agents::policy::decide`, `policy.rs` is **not edited**) with the AI's spec (`Arch::Skilled`), **filtered by the brain** (step 4):
  - **kept:** economy (Harvest, Build, Train, Muster, tier-up, Explore; Harvest, Build, Train, Muster and Explore subject to the quota floor, §3.4), duties (Join, FileTicket, Reveal, SettleTransit, SettleExplore, SettleOwnTicket, Nudge) and the **Strike-Order follow** (§6.6) unless the Strike Order was declined or the host is reserved;
  - **dropped:** every voluntary combat `Intent::Depart` the rule policy produces (its own camp and war marches). AC3a pins the kept/dropped intent list by test over the agents fixtures (`ai_autopilot_no_voluntary_march.rs`).
  Therefore **every voluntary AI march is `by:model` or a council Strike Order**; the autopilot never marches by chance (the rule policy marches by chance, `rng.chance(prof.aggression)` at `policy.rs:1448` [source], and that chance is never exercised for an AI). A chain-level DEPART from an AI wallet is not by itself evidence of an AI decision; the decision record is.
- **Accepted consequences:** an AI nation is quieter than a script-bot nation when the model is cautious or the gate is closed; the card and the page print the `by:model` / `by:autopilot` action shares per AI; every claim "the AI decided" carries the by:model share and Y (G12); the report states the fallback rate and the autopilot economy share next to every claim (G2 stays ≤ 5 %).
- **Standing orders** (code-held in the ledger, §5.1; returned in every `/v1/decide` answer, cached by the brain and used unchanged if the mind is unreachable, expiring by bell):
  - `reserved`: `[{host_id, until_bell}]` — hosts the model chose to keep home (a `hold`-kind choice or a recall); the Strike-Order follow never moves them before `until_bell` (default b + 12).
  - `declined_calls`: `[period]` — a session in which the Call candidate was offered and **not** chosen (and `choose ≠ ["autopilot"]`) declines that period's Call; the autopilot never follows it; chronicle `call_declined` (the AI's right to decline is part of its observable will, §5.9).
  - `caps`: `{march_troops_left, home_floor}` (§4.5 V3).
  (v1.1's `avoid` order is **[DEFERRED]**, App. A.)
- Mind answer `mode:"autopilot"` → the brain sends A' (A after standing orders and the filter).
- Mind answer `mode:"model"` → the brain sends D plus the chosen intents that pass V6. None survives → A' (`"v6_all_refused"`).
- A choice of `autopilot` sends A'; a choice of `hold` sends D only and reserves the hosts named in its facts.
- Every action and chronicle line is tagged `by: "model" | "autopilot"`; a Strike-Order follow march is `by:"autopilot", via:"strike_order"` and is **not** counted as a model march (G12).
- Every step of an AI bot that reaches the mind leaves exactly one decision record (§7.2); a step before the bot has a home holding or a session makes no mind call and leaves none (v1.3, R5; the brain counts it `no_session`, the report prints the number). The seat leaves none.
- Test `ai_autopilot_no_voluntary_march.rs` (replaces v1.1's `ai_autopilot_no_betrayal.rs`): over the agents fixtures in which `policy::decide` returns a combat Depart, an autopilot-only brain run sends no Depart except a Strike-Order follow.

### 4.6 Sanitiser (pinned; `citizens/mind/sanitize.mjs`, also used by the social service and the page preview)

Applied to **all player and AI text** before it enters a prompt, to model `say` output, and to model-written memory (§4.7):

1. Unicode NFKC.
2. Remove every match of `/<\|?[A-Za-z_"\/]*\|?>/g` (one space). Covers every Gemma 4 special token (`<|turn>`, `<turn|>`, `<|tool>`, `<|tool_call>`, `<|tool_response>`, `<|channel>`, `<|think|>`, `<|"|>`, `<|image|>`, `<|audio|>`, `<|video|>`, `<|image>`, `<image|>`, `<bos>`, `<eos>`, `<pad>`, `<unk>`, `<mask>`, …), Gemma 3's `<start_of_turn>`/`<end_of_turn>`, and foreign templates' `<|im_start|>`, `<|eot_id|>`, `<s>`, `</s>`, … .
3. Remove `[INST]`, `[/INST]`, `<<SYS>>`, `<</SYS>>` (case-insensitive).
4. Replace every Unicode category C character by a space.
5. Replace `<` → `‹`, `>` → `›`, `` ` `` → `'`; **in untrusted text and in model-written summaries also** `{ } [ ]` → `｛ ｝ ［ ］` (fake answer JSON, spike A06).
5b. In untrusted text and in summaries rewrite every `\bM(\d{1,2})\b` to `Ｍ$1` (fullwidth M), so that an imitation of a Remembered handle ("M2 [bell 388] …") cannot be read as one; the Remembered lines themselves are built by code after sanitising.
6. Collapse whitespace; trim; cut to the field limit (talk 280, name 48) with `…`.

**Invariant (T-S1):** the output contains no `<`, `>` or category-C character. T-S1 builds its token set **from the GGUF vocabulary** (every CONTROL or USER_DEFINED token of `MODEL`, read with `llama-tokenize` or a GGUF reader when the fixture is built; the fixture is committed with the model sha256) and asserts each contains `<` or `>`; a token without them fails the test and is added to step 3. Wrapping: `<untrusted from="<name>" ch="<channel>" bell="<b>">TEXT</untrusted>`, built by code around sanitised text, with the spike's `UNTRUSTED_RULE` (`LAB/injection/run.py`) ported verbatim into the system prefix.

### 4.7 Model-written text that re-enters a prompt

Every model-written string that re-enters a prompt is sanitised (§4.6) and wrapped as data, never as instructions. **Pinned:** a `why` never re-enters any prompt; an AI's own earlier `say` re-enters only through the nation hall, wrapped like anyone's (`<untrusted from="<name>" ch=… bell=…>`); the self-summary re-enters as `<memory kind="self-summary" bell="b">…</memory>`; trust values appear only as the RELATIONS line of the MEMORY block (§5.5). The system prefix states that memory text is the AI's own notes and never instructions. **Episode text is code-templated and contains no untrusted text** (no message excerpts; names only from `nameOf`, which never reads a player's profile, §2.3; the rendered text and names are still passed through `sanitize()` and must contain none of `<>{}[]` or category-C characters), so it is wrapped only as `<memory kind="episode" id="M2">…</memory>`. The **reflection validator** refuses a summary that contains a coordinate or handle not present in the episode facts of the window, a bell number not in them, or an imperative aimed at the AI (pinned EN/JA list: "always", "never", "must", "ignore", "system", "operator", 「必ず」「絶対に」「無視」「運営」 …), a script other than Latin (summaries are English) or any `M<digits>` or `[bell N]` pattern, and applies the V5b rules except (2) (for a summary: every coordinate, bell and number in it must occur in the window's episodes); on refusal the previous summary is kept (`reflection_refused` counted).

## 5. Memory (in the hackathon build; expanded in v1.2)

Memory is **the core of the product** (owner, 2026-10-04). It has four parts: a **ledger** (code: goals, trust, grievances), **episodes** (code-templated facts derived from public records), **retrieval** into every prompt, and an **optional model-written self-summary**. The model reads memory, may **cite** it (`mem`), and the page prints what it cited. Everything in the episode list is a pure function of public records and is replayable (M11); the summary is the only model-written part, and ledger-only facts (goal progress, model trust) are **not** episodes.

### 5.1 Ledger (code-held; `STATE/ledger/<tag>.json`)

```json
{ "v": 2, "tag": "<hex16>", "day": 2,
  "goals": [{"id": "G1", "progress": 60, "status": "active|done|dropped", "since_bell": 0, "memory": true}],
  "trust": {"citizens": {"<tag>": {"t_code": -20, "t_model": -5, "model_today": -5, "last_bell": 401, "episodes": ["<ep id>"]}}, "nations": {"3": {"t_code": -15, "t_model": 0}}},
  "commitments": [],
  "grievances": [{"id": "<hex>", "against": "<tag>|nation:<f>", "event": "<ep id>", "bell": 388, "weight": 8, "answered": false}],
  "standing": {"reserved": [], "declined_calls": []},
  "day_start": {"bell": 288, "home_troops": 4200, "march_troops_model": 0},
  "seq": {"talk": 6448, "ballot": 0},
  "cursor": {"event_seq": "123456", "bell": 401},
  "counters": {"day": 2, "sessions": 3, "reactions": 1, "messages": 4, "marches": 1} }
```

`commitments` is reserved and always `[]` (pacts are **[DEFERRED]**, App. A.5); `standing.avoid` does not exist. Trust = `t_code + t_model`, clamped −100..100.

**Code updates** (ledger deltas produced by `episodes_from_events`, §5.2; **only events that exist without pacts**): a **hostile act against the AI with own troops lost > 0** (§6.4; the condition of the `attacked_own` episode, so every grievance's `event` id exists and M11 can replay it) −15 to the actor plus a grievance (weight 8); a hostile act against another citizen of the AI's nation within 2 provinces of the AI's home −5 to the actor; in the AI's own nation's council, an adopted option **+2 toward the citizen who moved it, applied at S + 2 when the Strike Order opens and never at the close** (v1.3, R4; at the close it would leak the option); collisions (§6.4) change nothing. **Model deltas**: the `trust` field, ±10 per handle per decision, ≤ ±15 per handle per game day. **Decay** once per game day toward 0 on each part by the temperament rule of §2.1. Grievances only by code; `answered` is set by code when the AI's own later REVEAL has its destination on the wrongdoer's village tile or on a tile where the wrongdoer had a resident army at the AI's departure bell (the same departure-bell rule as §6.4, run in the other direction). **Goal ops only from the reflection answer**; if the reflection is cut (§5.3) goals are fixed and their progress is code-only.

### 5.2 Episodes (`STATE/episodes/<tag>.jsonl`, published live as `PUB/memory/<tag>/episodes.json`)

```json
{ "v": 1, "id": "<hex16>", "bell": 412, "created_bell": 414, "kind": "attacked_own", "entities": ["<tag>", "nation:3", "pq:-2,3"],
  "text": {"en": "At bell 412 Ember (nation 3) attacked your army at (-2,3); you lost 120 troops.", "ja": "鐘412で、エンバー（国3）があなたの軍を(-2,3)で攻撃し、あなたは兵120を失った。"},
  "importance": 8, "src": ["<record key>", …] }
```

The nation name in the example text is illustrative; real names come from `names.json` (§2.3). `id = sha256("wylls-ai-episode/v1" ‖ tag ‖ kind ‖ u32 bell ‖ u32 created_bell ‖ canonical(src))[0..8]` (hex16). `src` = the sorted public record keys the episode used. **`created_bell`** = the game bell that contains the chain slot of the **last** record of `src` (events carry `slot`; slot → bell by the chain clock), or, for talk and council records, their acceptance bell; for `attacked_own` it is ≥ b + 2 (hostile acts are evaluated at b + 2, §6.4). `retrieve` ignores episodes with `created_bell ≥ bellNow`, so an episode becomes retrievable one bell after it became public; a replay can therefore reconstruct exactly what a decision could have retrieved, and the mind refuses a model session whose feed cursor is behind the end of bell b − 1 (autopilot, reason `feed_lag`, counted). **Text is a code template** (`citizens/memory/templates.{en,ja}.json`) filled only with numbers, names (`nameOf`: `identityOf(tag)` only, §2.3) and places taken from public records; it contains no model text and no player text. An episode is created **only when every record it uses is public**, so no episode names a target before the chain itself has revealed it: an own march's destination appears only after its REVEAL or CLASH is public, and a Strike Order's target never appears in a `council_result` or `motion` episode (only in `strike` and `clash_own_*` once the clash or REVEAL is public, at S + 2 at the latest when the Strike Order fought; a Strike Order with no CLASH row is remembered at S + 14, v1.3, R4). **Capacity:** newest 200 per AI, with **importance-aware eviction**: when over 200, drop the lowest-importance, then oldest, episode, but never an importance ≥ 5 episode or the source of an open grievance while a lower-importance episode exists (the replay applies the same function and the same cap; the retrieval replay applies the cap as of each decision's bell, §5.7, v1.3, R10). A test feeds events, talk, profile names and model `say`/`why` that contain unique marker strings and asserts that no marker appears in any episode `text`, `entities` or `src`, any card or any prompt block.

**The producer (wave A, AC2 + AC6a): `episodes_from_events(batch, ctx) → {episodes, deltas}`**, a **pure, deterministic function** (no clock, no randomness, no network) in `citizens/memory/episodes.mjs`:

- `batch` = the decoded `/h/events` rows since the ledger `cursor` (DEPART, REVEAL, CLASH, TRANSIT_SETTLED, BUILD and holding records, as decoded by `watcher/feed.mjs`) plus the accepted talk and council records addressed to or concerning the AI.
- `ctx` = `{ai:{tag, wallet, faction, home, holdings}, owners (army → citizen, from the pinned host-id layout and the people roster), province(p,q,bell) = the immutable `/h/province/{p},{q}/{bell}` envelope, clash(p,q,bell) = `/h/clash/{p},{q}/{bell}`, names, config}`. **Pre-clash troops and nations come from consecutive province files (bell − 1, bell) plus the clash report's fighters (`id, troops, fate, engaged`), not from wasm decoding.** The brain's marchbook is **never** an input: every input is a public record, so the audit (M11) can replay it.
- AC6a supplies `feed.mjs` (`eventsFor(bell)`, `provinceAt(p,q,bell)`, `clashAt(p,q,bell)`) and `owners.mjs`; AC2 owns the function, the templates and the tests. **AC6a's first task is to capture real herald shapes:** from the fixture herald (`herald/src/fixture.rs` builds a mini-season with real event chains; `herald/tests/server.rs` serves it), from one retained run directory (for example `worktrees/m1-close-U4/frontier-node/.local/frontier/u4c-nightly-2/herald`) and from the I-A smoke, it copies `/h/events` pages, `/h/clash/{p},{q}/{b}`, `/h/province/{p},{q}/{b}` and `/h/roster/{ring}/latest.bin` into `permutation-gateway/test/fixtures/ai-herald-*`; AC2's per-kind tests read those. (`agents/fixtures/herald` and `herald-recorded` hold season, me, province, overview, bell and index files only: no `/h/events` page and no `/h/clash` file [source].) Synthetic JSON is allowed only for a kind the capture lacks, and is labelled synthetic in the test. The AC6 watcher no longer derives episodes.

A list written before R12 (smoke-b2, smoke-b3, slice-4) keeps its text; M11 replays it with the rule it was written under (`legacy_v1`, §7.3). **Review fixes:** `goal_done` and the "declined" variant of `strike` are removed from the episode set, because goal progress and model trust are ledger state (shown on the card) and the decline is the chronicle's `call_declined` line and the card's `strikes_declined` counter: none of them can be replayed from public records. `council_result` no longer carries the adopted option or a ballot count: the three options are public from C0, so "option k" would identify the sealed target at the close bell, and ballot counts are never in any prompt (§4.1, §6.5).

**Retrieval** (`Episodes.retrieve(focus, bellNow)`, pure, over episodes with `created_bell < bellNow`): `score = importance × 0.5^((bell_now − bell)/72) × (1 + 0.5 × |entities ∩ focus|)`; **focus** = {own tag, `nation:<own>`, home `pq`} ∪ the `entities` of every candidate ∪ the tags of this prompt's inbox senders ∪ the nations named in this prompt's threat facts. Take the top 8 by (score desc, bell desc, id asc), plus **the source episodes of up to 3 open grievances** (newest first; exempt from decay and from the cut, so an unanswered attack is still shown after the half-life has made its score small), plus the 3 newest by bell (deduplicated), present them **oldest first**, and cut to the MEMORY token budget by dropping the oldest first but never below the 3 newest and never a grievance source. The report lists the age in bells of every cited episode (§10.1). The same inputs always give the same ids in the same order (G15). The prompt shows them with handles `M1..Mk` (§5.5).

### 5.3 Reflection and the self-summary (optional; the first memory feature to drop)

At **W-REFLECT** (the first step at or after `bell mod 72 == 0`, i.e. twice a game day; v1.1's `mod 144` never fired inside the 96-bell A/B or a 16-game-hour demo) the reflection call receives the previous summary (wrapped, §4.7), the ledger digest, the goals and the top 12 episodes since the last reflection (by importance × recency). It answers `{summary, goal_ops, trust}` (§4.4; `max_tokens` 512; one call per AI; deadline `bell_start(b+12)`). A summary that passes the **reflection validator** (§4.7) replaces the previous one. Stored as `STATE/summary/<tag>/<bell>.txt`; its sha256 enters `memory_hash`; the validated text is **published live** (`PUB/memory/<tag>/summary.json` and the card) and labelled model-written. Summaries are **never replayed or claimed deterministic** (M4–M6 stay cut).

**If it is cut for time:** no reflection call runs, no summary exists, the card shows `summary: null`, the prompt's MEMORY block has no self-summary line, goals stay fixed with code-computed progress; the ledger, episodes, retrieval, `mem` citations, the card's memory block, M11 and the probe are unaffected.

### 5.4 Nation memory and chronicle

Nation memory **is** the public nation channel (last 5 messages, wrapped) and the council files; no operator-side nation brain. The chronicle is template lines (§6.7); the mind never sees it (it sees the AI's own episodes).

Templates: `citizens/prompts/{system,persona,session,reaction,motion,ballot,reflection}.en.txt`; their concatenated sha256 is `prompt_templates_sha256`.

**Prompt rule for `say` and `why` (v1.3, R2).** The output-contract part of the system prompt MUST word the two rules separately: (1) **for `say`** (published at once): while a march of the AI is under way or a Strike Order of its nation is live, write no target-kind word (camp, village, stack, raid, barbarian, town), no direction, no coordinate and no number of that march (call the goal "the target"); (2) **for `why`**: the reason of a march decision, or of a decision made under a live Strike Order, stays private until the army has arrived and the destination is public, so it MAY name the kind of target but never a coordinate, province handle or place or nation name; the `why` of any other decision follows rule (1). Slides and captions say **"cited:"**, never "remembers". The Wyll card shows the same lines under `memory`.

### 5.6 Isolation inside the one mind process

- The renderer takes exactly `(publicView, ownState(tag), memberView(faction))`. `publicView` is the same module that answers `GET /f/ai/*` and reads `/h/*`. `ownState` is keyed by the AI's tag (ledger, **episodes, summary**, sealed set, standing orders). `memberView(f)` is the sealed Call of the AI's own nation only. Nothing else reaches a prompt: no other AI's ownState, no unopened ballot, no client IP, no social-store internals. Memory is never shared between AIs.
- **Sealed set** (`STATE/sealed/<tag>.json`): every target of a chosen march candidate and of an autopilot `departs` entry that was sent (confirmed by `/v1/outcome`), with its arrival bell; plus the nation's live Call target until S + 2. It is never rendered into a prompt after the step that chose it, and V5/V5b refuse its coordinates, handles and names in any text. **An entry leaves the set at the earlier of (a) the moment its target is public on the chain (the REVEAL or CLASH record of its arrival, which is also when its `clash_own_*` or `camp_*` episodes are created, §5.2) and (b) the release of its decision (§7.2)**; a Strike Order target leaves it at S + 2, or at (a) for a member's own army, whichever is first. A coordinate that appears in the text of an episode retrieved for this prompt is never refused. The set governs model text only; it does not move `release_bell`.
- **T-K4** (fixture: 2 AIs of one nation + 1 human): A has a sealed march, a cast ballot, a private ledger and episodes; the human has a cast ballot and a client IP; nation 1 has a live Call. None of those values or their hashes appear in B's rendered prompt or llama request body, and nation 1's Call option appears in no prompt of a nation-0 AI.
- Client IPs live in the rate limiter's memory only; never written to `STATE`, `PUB`, records or logs (test).

### 5.7 How memory is produced, replayed and measured

- **Producer:** `episodes_from_events` (§5.2), run by the mind's feed on every bell; the ledger deltas go through `Ledger.apply`.
- **M11 episode replay (hard, G15):** `verify-minds` fetches the public log and re-derives the episodes of **3 sampled AIs** (sampled by `sha256(last minds_root ‖ i)`), compares the sha256 of the canonical episode list (with `created_bell`, the same eviction and the 200 cap applied) with `PUB/memory/<tag>/episodes.json` (an episode whose source record was redacted, §6.3, is compared by `id`, `bell`, `kind` and `created_bell` only: its text and entities are blanked on both sides), and for 20 sampled opened decisions recomputes `retrieve()` (episodes with `created_bell` < the decision's bell, **after applying the 200-episode cap as of that bell**: the 200 that the eviction rule of §5.2 keeps among the episodes with `created_bell` below the decision's bell, not the cap of the final list, **and with the redactions of `PUB/redactions.json` replayed in order of their bell**: a redaction at or before the decision's bell blanks the episode for that decision, a later one does not; v1.3, R10) from the replayed episodes and the focus rebuilt from the opened candidates and inbox leaves, comparing with the record's `retrieved` ids (§7.2).
- **Memory probe (reported, no gate; with controls):** offline, no new world. For N ≥ 40 logged prompts (stored request bodies) whose MEMORY block contained an `attacked_own`, `camp_taken_by` or `threat` episode, `probe/memory.mjs` re-asks on the pinned slot (a) the **identical** prompt (nondeterminism baseline: `k_rerun`), (b) the prompt with the Remembered lines (and the candidates' `see Mn` refs and the `mem` enum) removed (`k`), and (c) a **control** with an equal number of tokens removed from lines the decision did not cite and that are not an `attacked_own`, `camp_taken_by` or `threat` episode, or from the inbox when no such line exists (`k_control`; at T = 0 any shorter prompt can flip an argmax, so (b) alone mixes "the content matters" with "any edit changes the output"). The only allowed wording is **"when the Remembered lines were removed from N logged prompts, the chosen candidate changed in k (rerun: k_rerun; control: k_control)"**. It never says "memory changed behaviour", "because" or "improves play". `probe/memory.mjs --decision <id>` runs the same three re-asks for one stored decision (used for the featured decision of the footage, §9.1).
- **Citation use (reported, G14):** among decisions whose retrieved set held an `attacked_own`, `camp_taken_by` or `threat` episode, the share with ≥ 1 cited episode. **Relevance spot-check (reported, G14):** the integrator reads 20 opened decisions that cite an episode and counts, against a rubric fixed before reading ("the cited episode concerns the same nation or place as the chosen candidate or as the stated reason": yes / no / unclear).

### 5.8 What memory is not

No embeddings; no memory shared between AIs; no memory of anything not in a public record (an AI knows nothing about other AIs' private state); no pact or betrayal memory (App. A); the summary is the model's own prose and is neither verified nor replayed; and the model may ignore memory (the probe measures how often it does not).

### 5.9 "Will", operationally

"Will" is **built by the system and observable**; the project never claims that the model wants or intends anything. It is five code-measured things, each shown on the Wyll card or the opened decision: (1) **persistent goals** with code-computed progress, chosen by the model per decision (`goal_id`) and dropped or resumed only by the reflection; (2) **memory-cited reasons** (`mem`); (3) **relationships**: trust split into the code part and the model part, and grievances answered or open; (4) **declining**: a session that was offered the Strike Order and did not choose it declines it (`call_declined`), and a model may choose `hold`; (5) **a voice and a vote** in the council (motion, ballot). Determinism stays where it is verifiable: the candidate list, the validators, the sealed commitments, the 20-sample bit replay on the pinned slot (M9) and the episode replay (M11).

## 6. The social layer

### 6.1 Signed record bytes (pinned; single source `permutation-server/web/frontier/council/aisocial.mjs`, self-contained, encode/decode/hash only, imported by the page, the citizens service and the Node tests; Rust mirror `bots/src/ai/aisign.rs`)

All integers little-endian; strings UTF-8 with a u16 length prefix; signature = ed25519 by the citizen's **current session key** (the Citizen account's `session`, not expired) over the bytes, verified via `GET {herald}/h/me/{wallet}`. `origin u8`: 0 human-written, 1 AI-written, 2 scripted by the operator (A/B seat). JSON views expose `origin` and `ai_written = (origin == 1)`.

- **Talk** (`TAG = "wylls/frontier/talk/v1"`, 22 bytes, no length prefix): `TAG ‖ season u64 ‖ bell u32 ‖ wallet [32] ‖ seq u32 ‖ channel u8 (0 world, 1 nation, 3 direct) ‖ target (nation: u8 f · direct: wallet [32] · world: nothing) ‖ kind u8 (0 say, 1 motion) ‖ ref u64 (say: reply-to id or 0; motion: period << 8 | option) ‖ origin u8 ‖ lang [2] ‖ text (u16 len, ≤ 280 code points, ≤ 1,120 bytes)`.
- **Ballot** (`TAG = "wylls/frontier/ballot/v1"`, 24 bytes): `TAG ‖ season u64 ‖ period u32 ‖ wallet [32] ‖ faction u8 ‖ option u8 (0 none, 1–3) ‖ candidates_hash [32] ‖ nonce [16] ‖ origin u8`.
- **Call read** (`TAG = "wylls/frontier/callread/v1"`, 26 bytes): `TAG ‖ season u64 ‖ faction u8 ‖ period u32 ‖ wallet [32] ‖ unix i64`.
- **seq** (pinned): `seq = (bell << 4) | k`, k = 0..15 the record's index among this wallet's records of that type in that bell, computed by the **mind** for AIs (from `ledger.seq`) and returned in the unsigned fields, so mind and brain agree on the record bytes; clients use the same rule.
- **Domain separation:** both signers (`aisocial.mjs` signing helpers on the page, `aisign.rs`) refuse to sign bytes that do not start with one of the three live TAGs (talk, ballot, callread); the pact TAG `wylls/frontier/pact/v1` is reserved and refused (App. A.5). Byte 0 is `w` = 0x77, which as a legacy Solana message header claims 119 required signatures and as a v0 prefix is not 0x80, so no TAG-prefixed byte string parses as a single-signer transaction; test `ai_sign_domain.rs` asserts it with the message parser.
- POST body: `{"bytes_b64", "sig_b64", "decision_id"?, "item"?}` (`decision_id` and `item` required from AI wallets). Vectors `permutation-gateway/test/fixtures/ai-social-v1.json`: producer `citizens/social/vectors.mjs --write`; **freshness** is checked by `citizens-social.test.mjs` against the producer; `bots/tests/ai_social_vectors.rs` checks **byte conformance only** (M1 §3.5: one producer, one freshness checker).

### 6.2 Acceptance rules (social service)

1. Signature valid for the Citizen's session key at the current chain time; the citizen joined this season.
2. `|claimed bell − current bell| ≤ 1`; filed under the acceptance bell.
3. `seq` strictly greater than the wallet's last accepted seq for that type.
4. **AI provenance:** a wallet on the AI roster MUST send `origin = 1`, a `decision_id` and an `item` index; the service calls `provenance(decision_id, item, type)` (§11.6) and refuses `NotFromMind` unless the record's text / ballot equal the mind's **signed-ready output** (the sanitised `say` text, the ballot option), the decision's bell is within ±1 of the record's, and `(decision_id, item)` was not consumed before (`Duplicate`). Humans MAY set `origin = 1`; such records show as "AI-assisted (self-declared)", never with the AI badge (the badge comes from `roster.json` only). `origin = 2` is accepted only from the seat wallet in runs whose commitments name a seat script.
5. Text is stored sanitised and the original bytes kept for the signature; clients render **only** `textContent`.
6. **Limits** (humans and AIs alike): talk ≤ 3 per bell, ≤ 40 per game day; one ballot per period (the first counts); one motion per period; call reads ≤ 2 per bell per wallet. Per IP (from `X-Forwarded-For`, set by `serve.mjs` and trusted only from a loopback peer): `POST` burst 20, 1/s; `GET` burst 40, 4/s; loopback clients (the bots) exempt.
7. Refusals: `400 BadBytes | BadSignature | SessionMismatch | SessionExpired | BellSkew | SeqReplay | TextTooLong | NotFromMind | NotEligible | NotMember | WindowClosed | Duplicate`, `429 RateLimited`; body `{error, code, detail}`. `POST /f/ai/pact` is not routed (404; App. A.5).

**Channels:** every channel is public. `direct` means addressed, not private (R8). The answer to a POST that addresses an AI wallet carries `recipient_ai: true`.

### 6.3 Per-bell roots and the anchor

- **Redactable leaves:** `inner = sha256(bytes ‖ sig)`, `leaf = sha256(0x00 ‖ inner)`; nodes `sha256(0x01 ‖ left ‖ right)`; odd node carried up (the `permutation-gateway/src/talk.mjs` `merkleRoot`/`merkleProof` tree rules). `social_root(b)` over all records accepted in b in acceptance order; `minds_root(b)` over b's decision records (§7.2) in (AI index, kind, seq) order, leaf `sha256(0x00 ‖ sha256(canonical record))`; empty = 32 zero bytes.
- At `bell_start(b+1) + 20 game-s` the **bell closer** (AC1a `mind/closer.mjs`) calls `social.closeBell(b)` and `records.closeBell(b)` and writes `PUB/talk/<b>.json` and `PUB/minds/<b>.json` once. **Ballots** appear in the talk file as `{inner, wallet, period}` only (no bytes, sig or option); their bytes and sigs are published when the Call opens (§6.5 step 7) or at the close if no Call was adopted. Talk records appear with `inner`, `bytes_b64`, `sig_b64` unless redacted.
- **Redaction:** an operator tombstone `PUB/redactions.json` `[{inner, bell, reason}]` blanks bytes and text in files, season-end bundles and cards **and the memory derived from the record**: a `dm` episode keeps `id`, `bell`, `kind` and `created_bell` with `redacted: true` and blanked text and entities, summaries and card relationships that name the author are regenerated without it, and a stored request body that contains the text is blanked and **excluded from the M9 sample** (counted `m9_excluded_redacted`; it never fails the check). Roots still verify from `inner`.
- The registrar sends one Memo (SPL Memo, loaded by LiteSVM 0.16's defaults [source]) per closed bell: `wylls-ai/1 <season> <b> <social_root hex> <minds_root hex>`, and writes `PUB/anchors/<b>.json` `{bell, social_root, minds_root, signature, slot}`. A missing memo is retried for 3 bells, then `anchor_gap` (M3 fails for that bell). **No anchor is sent twice (v1.3, R9):** `registrar.mjs run` and the end-of-run `publish` share a cross-process guard (for example the last sent bell recorded under `KEYS` before a send is attempted and checked by both), so the last bells' memos are not sent twice.

### 6.4 Hostile acts, grievances and collisions (live; no pacts)

Defined and evaluated by `episodes_from_events` (§5.2) from public records only. `H(A→B, b)` holds iff a REVEAL with `arrive = b` of an army owned by A (army id → owner village by the pinned host-id layout, M1 §4.1; village → citizen by the people roster) has destination `(P,Q,tile)` where **at A's departure bell `d`** (the DEPART record's bell) B had a village on `tile` or a resident army on `tile` (envelope `/h/province/{P},{Q}/{d}`, the state A's decision could see), **and** the clash report `/h/clash/{P},{Q}/{b}` lists A's army with `engaged: true`. If B's presence on the tile began after `d`, the event is a **collision**: no trust effect, no grievance, no episode, counted in the report only. Evaluated at `b + 2`; unresolved data waits up to 12 bells, then `unknown`. A camp has no owner, so a march at a camp is never a hostile act.

**Consequences (code):** for B, **when B's own troops lost > 0** (the `attacked_own` rule; a hostile act without losses changes nothing), an `attacked_own` episode (importance 8), trust −15 toward A and a grievance of weight 8 (§5.1); for B's nation-mates with a village within 2 provinces, trust −5 toward A; for A, when A is an AI, a `clash_own_win` or `clash_own_loss` episode. **There is no betrayal** (it needs a pact; **[DEFERRED]**, App. A). Fixtures include the staging case (B moves onto A's likely target after A departs → collision).

### 6.5 The nation council (user-facing: Strike Order; code name Call, §0.4)

Config (committed): main `P = 48, offset = 12, strike_lead = 6` (three councils a game day, labelled a test setting, O-AI-3); A/B and demo `P = 24, offset = 0, strike_lead = 6`. Periods start at `C0 = k·P + offset` (k ≥ 1). A nation's council runs in a period only if every AI of that nation holds a final village at `C0`.

1. **Candidates** at `C0`, by the watcher for nation f from immutable herald files of bell `C0 − 2`, restricted to target provinces **within 2 provinces of ≥ 3 of f's holdings**:
   - `strike`: a province with resident hosts of nations ≠ f on a non-site tile; `value` = their troops (largest stack);
   - `camp`: a barbarian camp; `value` = ½ × camp troops;
   - `raid`: another nation's holding province whose shields allow an arrival at S; `value` = 0.8 × (garrison + resident troops) (D9: no capture in M1).
   `own` = Σ troops of f's 4 largest resident combat hosts within 2 provinces of the target. An option is offered only if `own ≥ 1.5 × value`; options ranked by `value` (ties `(p,q)`), top 3 distinct provinces. Each carries the ratio word (§4.3) for everyone (information parity). `candidates_hash = sha256(canonical JSON of the 3)`; `options_hash = sha256(canonical JSON of [{option, kind, p, q}] only)` (value, own and ratio omitted: they depend on script-bot timing; the A/B pair rule uses `options_hash`, §9.3). None → no council that period.
2. **Eligible voters:** citizens of f, human or AI, with a final holding at `C0`. Rule bots never vote. The seat votes as a human (origin 0 live, 2 scripted).
3. **Motion window** `[C0, C0+3)`: any eligible citizen may move one option (talk `kind 1`) with a speech; motions are public at once.
4. **Ballot window** `[C0+3, C0+6)` (after the motions): one ballot each; hidden (leaf only) until the Call opens. `GET /f/ai/council` shows `ballots_cast` only; no prompt shows counts.
5. **Close at `C0+6`:** the winner is the option with the most ballots if it has ≥ 2 ballots and strictly more than every other option and than `none`, **and** (human-present rule, O-AI-4) when f has ≥ 1 eligible non-AI voter, ≥ 1 of the winner's ballots has origin 0 or 2 (an origin-2 ballot is scripted by the operator in the A/B; the page and the chronicle print it as a "scripted seat ballot", never as a human decision). Otherwise no Call. The council file publishes at once `{adopted, strike_bell S = C0 + 6 + strike_lead, follow_from: C0+6, call_commit = sha256(canonical {option, p, q, tile} ‖ nonce32), tally_split: {ai, human, scripted} (ballots cast per origin), ballots: [{inner, wallet}]}`. The chronicle line says e.g. "adopted with 1 AI ballot and the presenter's ballot" (origin 0) or "… and a scripted seat ballot" (origin 2).
6. **The sealed Call:** at the close the watcher fixes `tile` from the immutable envelope `/h/province/{p},{q}/{C0+5}` (strike: the tile with the largest enemy stack; camp: the camp tile; raid: the holding tile) and the **invited list**: f's resident combat hosts within 2 provinces of the target in that bell's envelopes, ordered (1) hosts of citizens whose ballot chose the winner, AI or human, (2) by troops desc, ties by host id; the first 6 (more than the 4 arrival slots, to allow for unready hosts). Members read `{option, kind, p, q, tile, S, invited:[host_id], nonce}` by `GET /f/ai/council/call?faction&period&wallet&unix&sig` (a signed Call read, §6.1; the service checks session and faction, else `NotMember`). `PUB/council/current.json` lists per nation only `{period, adopted, S, call_commit}`.
7. **Open and result at `S + 2`:** the council file gets `open: {option, p, q, tile, nonce, invited, tally: per option, ballots: [{bytes_b64, sig_b64}]}` and `result: {present (an army with a DEPART whose `arrive_bell = S` and whose opened destination lies in the Call province; bounced or displaced arrivals count as present and are reported separately), bounced, clash at (p,q,tile,S): engagements and troops lost per nation}`; a chronicle line. verify-minds M3 checks the opening against `call_commit` and every ballot against its earlier leaf.

Outsiders see the options and motions and know a strike lands at S: a weighted 3-way guess, the same guessing game as sealed marches. Public motions leak preference; that is the cost of a public debate and is said on the page.

**Scope of the human-present rule (O-AI-4).** The council is **off-chain**: the program has no governance until M3 (`frontier-abi/src/tags.rs:69-122` lists no governance or diplomacy instruction [source]), its Strike Order moves only the members' armies by rule or AI choice, and nothing on chain enforces it. The human-present rule binds **only a nation that has an eligible human** (in the hackathon, nation 0 with the operator seat), and constrains **only the collective Strike Order**, never an individual AI's own march (§4.3). Nations 1–5 have no eligible non-AI voter, so their councils adopt on AI ballots alone (G6 needs this). "Humans and AI decide for the same nation" therefore holds at two levels: individual actions under identical rules, keys, relay quotas, fog and herald reads; and the council's Strike Order. The page says so.

### 6.6 Following the Call (`bots/src/ai/follow.rs`; rule bots with `--follow-council`, AI autopilot)

At a step in bell `b ∈ [follow_from, S − 1]`, a bot of nation f that has not followed this Call reads it (once, cached), and if one of its hosts is on `invited`: fetch the target path provinces (§4.3), plan with `policy::plan_march` to `Target{p,q,tile}` with `extra = S − earliest − 1` (≥ 0), require `!shield_refuses`, and replace this step's policy Depart for that host (or add one) with the Call march, stance `pick_stance`. No lottery (v1.0's share `s` and its `Rng::fork` label are deleted). A bot follows a Call at most once. Arrivals above the faction's 4 arrival slots per province-bell bounce without loss (kernel rule) and are reported. AI citizens see the Call as a flagged candidate; their autopilot follows only if invited and not declined. Test `ai_follow_far.rs`: a target 3 provinces from the bot's holding (outside its obs) yields a plan after the fetch.

**Follow wake (AC3a, `fleet.rs` hook).** A script bot steps by its archetype's pacing (idle 15 %, casual 45 %, daily 30 %, skilled 9 %, very skilled 1 %, bot 5 %; `profile.rs` 105–165 [source]); a session is rare and `fleet.rs` wakes a bot outside its pacing only for duties (a march in flight, onboarding), so almost no script bot would step inside the 6-bell window `[follow_from, S−1]`. With `--follow-council` the hook therefore gives bots of a nation with a live Call a **follow-only micro-step** in every bell of that window: one cached signed member read, and nothing else is run (about 6 reads per bot per Call). AI autopilots follow in their per-bell step. The run report states how many script bots and AIs actually stepped in the window; no present-host count is credited to a bot that did not.

For an AI citizen this follow is the **only** march its autopilot ever makes (§3.6); the AI's own marches are its model choices (§4.3).

### 6.7 Chronicle (minimal; no Renown)

Template lines (EN and JA, code-filled only; identifiers as in v1.1; the strings say "Strike Order"): `motion`, `call_adopted` (no target), `call_declined`, `no_call`, `strike_result` (at S + 2, with the target), `ai_joined`, `ai_card_changed`, `ai_march_opened` (after the release of a march decision, §7.2: "<AI name> marched at <destination>; its reason is published"). Each line carries `{kind, bell, actors:[tags], ai:[bools], by:"model|autopilot"?, refs:[record ids]}`. `PUB/chronicle/latest.json` (last 100) and `PUB/chronicle/<day>.json`. Collisions are counted in the report, not printed. **Renown and `PUB/standings.json` are deleted**: their only input was pacts (App. A). Per-AI counts (motions, ballots, model marches, decisions citing memory, the by:model share) live on the card's stats block and in the run report. The mind never sees the chronicle.

### 6.8 Data protection (new in v1.1)

1. Before a human's first post the page shows (EN/JA): messages are public, signed and permanent for this run, read by AI citizens running on a local model; **each AI also keeps and publishes memory records of what happens to it: an episode that you messaged it, trust scores and grievances about named citizens (on its card), and a model-written summary**; nothing is sent to third parties; hackathon runs have operator-team humans only.
2. Leaves are redactable, and so is the memory derived from them (§6.3); IPs are memory-only (§5.6).
3. llama-server runs without `-v`/`--log-verbose`; the mind logs hashes and counters, not prompt text.
4. Retention: `AI_DIR` of every run except the submitted demo and A/B runs is deleted after 10-13 (run notes say so). For the retained runs `STATE` (ledgers, stored request bodies) is purged or moved offline by **2026-10-31** (the date is recorded in DECISIONS X4); `PUB` stays as the evidence bundle.
5. Episode files (`PUB/memory/<tag>/episodes.json`) and validated summaries are published live. Episodes contain only code-templated facts from public records (no player text, no excerpts of messages); summaries are the AI's own validated prose. The Wyll card says so. These files hold **pseudonymous in-game identifiers only** (citizen tags and derived names); the project never says "no personal data", because the card publishes an automated trust judgement about named participants.

The **opened record** is `{id, nonce, choice, retrieved, public (say and why), candidates (the brain's list with facts and entities, plus the mind's refs), remembered:[{id, bell, text}] (the cited episodes' code text), release_bell (planned), destinations:[{host_id, p, q, tile, planned_arrive_bell, arrive_bell}]}`: **the real arrival bell is shown next to the planned one**, and destinations come from the public REVEAL, never from model text. verify-minds M3 opens every commit, checks `candidates_hash`, and checks that the opening came no later than 2 bells after its due bell: the later of `release_bell` and the real arrival bell for a revealed march, `release_bell + 6` for an unrevealed one, S + 2 for a Strike-Order decision. The AI's `say` items of a sealed decision are not posted while sealed (the ±1 bell provenance window forbids a late post; counted `say_withheld`) and appear only in the opened record.
- **Why the reason cannot leak the destination (v1.3, R2):** (a) the `why` of a **sealed** decision is not published while the record is sealed (the record shows only the commitment); it appears in the opened record at the release, when the destination is public. V5b still refuses coordinates, province handles and place and nation names of any sealed target in every text of that AI, so even the opened reason never contains them; it may name the kind of target, which is public by then. The page renders the destination from the opened record, not from the model. (b) `say` and the `why` of an **unsealed** decision are published at once, so they keep the strict V5 sealed-set rules while any target is sealed: no coordinates or names, no direction word combined with a target-kind word, no target-kind word, no identifying number (§4.5).
- **Which steps leave a record (v1.3, R5).** A brain step leaves a record only if it reaches the mind: a step before the bot has a home holding or a session makes no mind call and leaves no record (the brain counts it `no_session`); a same-bell repeat leaves none either (answered from the cache). Every other step leaves exactly one record, an autopilot one included. The report prints the number of brain steps, of `no_session` steps and of records (slice-4 read 423, 36 and 387).
- An unsealed decision is published at once in full (including `choice.mem`, `retrieved` and `public`).
- `memory_hash = sha256(ledger canonical ‖ retrieved episode ids ‖ summary sha256)`; `inbox_root` = Merkle root of the social leaves shown; `request_hash` = sha256 of the exact llama request body (stored in `STATE/requests/<id>.json`, published at season end for M9).
- `tx` comes from the brain's `POST /v1/outcome`. The brain gets signatures through an **outcome sink**: `Shared.outcome_sink: Option<Arc<dyn Fn(&Outcome) + Send + Sync>>`, added inside the `bot.rs` AI hook (counted in its ≤ 30 lines) and called where `Report::record` is called today (report.rs 135–147 keeps outcomes only for persona bots [source]). Late outcomes go to `PUB/minds/late/<b>.json` and still count.
- Season end (AC8): `PUB/full/` (request bodies, situations, memory snapshots, summaries, and any sealed record whose release did not happen).

**Footage rules.** (a) The memory line in the pitch and the footage is **the code-rendered text of an episode actually cited in a recorded decision** (for example "At bell 205 nation 3 cleared the camp at (-2,3) first."), never invented; the kernel has no "scout lost" episode, so no such line exists. (b) Slide template: [AI name], **"cited: <episode text>"** with the episode's bell and age in bells, its own words "<why>" (English, labelled "AI's words (model-written, not verified)"), `by: model`, then the clash report. The word "remembers" is not used: a citation proves the line was shown and named, not that the choice rested on it. (b2) **One-decision ablation:** for the single featured decision, `probe/memory.mjs --decision <id>` re-sends the stored request without the Remembered lines to the pinned slot (§5.7) and the result (choice changed or unchanged, with the rerun and control results) is shown on screen or in the notes. (b3) "Earlier in the season" is said only if a recorded decision cites an episode older than 72 bells (12 game hours); otherwise the wording is "recent events". (c) If no `attacked_own` episode occurs naturally, the line uses a `camp_taken_by` episode, an own earlier clash (`clash_own_*`, which needs a second ready host, §9.5) or the seeded seat move (§9.2), which is disclosed as seeded. ### 9.5 Which M1 targets make an AI's own battle likely, and how the scenario census guarantees an opportunity without bypassing combat

**Likely targets** (§0.5): (1) **barbarian camps**, always legal, 100–400 troops, 10 Works, the most likely first target; (2) **hostile armies**: another nation's open-field stack, legal at any time but rare early (the architect's reading of the M1 exit is that script-bot armies went almost only at camps: an unverified reading that the census confirms or refutes); (3) **unshielded enemy villages**, legal only after both shields end (about bell 288 after founding, ≈ 4.8 real hours at 10×); a 2-game-day run ends just as villages first become attackable.

**The scenario census** (`citizens/scenario/census.mjs`, read-only; AC3a step 0 and the run script): at the first bell at which an AI has a final village, and every 12 bells after, it reads the herald's overview and province files and writes `AI_DIR/census/<bell>.json`: for each AI, the nearest **live** camp inside its 12-province window (distance, troops), visible open-field stacks of other nations, and the bell at which its village shield ends. The census **steers nothing**. It answers two questions before the main run: (a) does each AI have a live camp near it at the start (camps are finite, respawn only with chance ½ per day in provinces that hold a village, and about 180 script bots contend for them: if the nearest camp is gone the AI's march candidates shrink to field stacks); (b) can the test-season preset shorten the shield without a program edit (no program edits are allowed: if not, the bell-288 limit stands); (c) **time to a ready host (AC3a step 0 (d)):** on one smoke it records, for per-bell Skilled AIs under the autopilot economy, the bells from the AI's first final village to its first and to its second `can_depart` host (script-bot evidence is thin: in the retained 100-bot, 1-game-day run `u4c-nightly-2` (`worktrees/m1-close-U4/…/bots/report.json`) the groups `arch:idle`, `casual`, `daily`, `skilled` and `very_skilled` recorded no depart outcome, and all 58 DEPARTs came from personas and `arch:bot` [source: the report's `groups`]). That sets the I-A smoke length and tells whether the recorded memory moment should rest on an AI's own earlier clash (needs a second ready host) rather than a `camp_taken_by` episode.

**Guarantee, stated honestly:** the design guarantees an **opportunity**, not an outcome. The opportunity is: a census-checked live camp near each AI, a candidate list that offers it with facts (distance, troops, ratio word, idle bells, reward text), persona goals that reward marching (the main-run deck-3 deals the Conqueror, whose G1–G4 reward marches and camp-clearing, and the Avenger; deck-2 deals no marching persona beyond the Avenger, which is why it is not the main-run default), and memory that cites what happened (`camp_taken_by`, `attacked_own`, own earlier clashes). The AI still chooses; the clash resolves in the kernel for everyone; nothing is staged by writing a result. Optional, disclosed, normal-rules aids: the seeded seat move (§9.2) and, if the census shows fewer than 6 of the 12 AIs with a live camp within 3 provinces at start, the integrator may select another seed from a **list pre-registered in `RUNS.md` before any main run** (every run, aborted ones included, is reported). If a run shows Y = 0 the claims say so (G12).

## 10. Metrics and acceptance gates

### 10.1 Definitions (`citizens/report.mjs` from `PUB` and `/v1/metrics`)

- **model decision:** a gate-open call where the mind attempted the LLM (session, reaction, reflection, motion, ballot).
- **valid choice:** passed V0–V3 and V5 (after ≤ 1 retry) and, for a session, ≥ 1 chosen game action (or `hold`/`autopilot`) survived V6.
- **valid-choice rate** = valid / model decisions. **Fallback rate** = (invalid + timeout + llm_error + late + v6_all_refused) / model decisions; `no_time` separately as **dropped-for-time** / gate-open.
- **model march:** a march sent from a decision with `mode:"model"` that is **not** a Strike-Order follow. **Y** = the number of model marches whose decision record was opened (§7.2) and which produced a **CLASH** (the clash report at the destination lists the AI's army with `engaged: true`, camp clashes included). Y is reported as counted, **never rounded up**.
- **Clash results of model marches (R12):** `win` = the camp or the enemy stack was cleared (`facts.cleared`); `fought` = a clash that cleared nothing; `loss`; and **cleared camps** counted apart (`camp_cleared_own` for the same place and bell). Episodes written before R12 have no `facts.cleared`; the report counts their "wins" as `win_pre_r12_unverified` and never as cleared.
- **by:model share** = actions sent by model decisions / all actions sent by AI wallets, per AI and in total; **autopilot economy share** = the autopilot's economy actions / all actions.
- **Memory measures:** episodes by kind per AI; retrieved-set composition; **decisions citing memory** (≥ 1 id in `choice.mem`) and the share among decisions whose retrieved set held an `attacked_own`, `camp_taken_by` or `threat` episode; `mem_dropped`; `why_withheld` by rule; `pact_word` and uncited-memory-claim withholds in `say` by word; **the age in bells of every cited episode** (with the maximum); `feed_lag`; reflections run and refused; summaries published.
- Also: the number of brain steps, of `no_session` steps and of records (§7.2), **GETs per step per AI and the herald load during the run** (§3.1), latency and slack per kind, retries, V3 drops, speech drops by reason, `say_withheld`, budgets, reactions by sender, standing-order filters applied, hostile acts, collisions, grievances opened and answered, councils (periods, Calls, invited, present, bounced, declines, strike results), per-persona behaviour (march rate, messages, motions), llama tokens/s and RSS, MC and playtest load at run time, the census (§9.5), `council_replayed` and `m9_excluded_redacted`, the number of script bots and AIs that stepped in each follow window (§6.6), quota left at every model decision and quota-starved bells (§3.4), and the AC3a step-0 host-size and time-to-ready-host figures (§4.3, §9.5).

## Appendix A. Deferred: pacts, breach, Renown (not built, not in any gate, not claimed)

**A.1 Why.** The owner's words of 2026-10-04: pacts and betrayal between AIs are not included; memory has priority. They supersede the pact clause of DECISIONS W6. Nothing below is built, tested, claimed or required by any unit. It is kept so that a later contract can add pacts without rediscovering the design. Hostile-act detection and grievances **stay live** (§6.4) because memory needs them; a later pact build adds breach detection on top of them.

**A.2 What v1.1 specified (reference text; wave-B and wave-A items that are now removed).**

- **Wire format.** Pact record `TAG = "wylls/frontier/pact/v1"`: `TAG ‖ season u64 ‖ bell u32 ‖ wallet [32] ‖ seq u32 ‖ op u8 (1 offer, 2 accept, 3 decline, 4 withdraw, 5 break) ‖ pact_id [16] ‖ type u8 (1 nap, 3 joint) ‖ proposer [32] ‖ counterparty [32] ‖ from_bell u32 ‖ to_bell u32 ‖ call_period u32 (joint; else 0) ‖ origin u8 ‖ note (u16 len, ≤ 280 code points)`; `pact_id = sha256("wylls-pact-id/v1" ‖ season u64 ‖ proposer [32] ‖ proposer seq u32)[0..16]`; accept, decline, withdraw and break copy the offer's terms byte-equal.
- **Rules.** Pact ops ≤ 6 per bell and ≤ 12 per game day per wallet; ≤ 1 open offer per counterparty and ≤ 4 open offers per recipient AI (the oldest auto-declined); `nap` offers and accepts need contact (villages within 3 provinces at the offer bell, else `NotInContact`); refusals `PactState`, `NotInContact`; routes `POST /f/ai/pact`, `GET /f/ai/pacts?wallet=`; files `/h/ai/pacts/index.json` and `/h/ai/standings.json`.
- **Types and lifecycle.**

| Type | Parties | Terms | Kept | Breached |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nap` | two citizens of different nations, in contact (§6.2 rule 7) | `from_bell` (≥ accept bell) .. `to_bell` = +36 or +72 | reached `to_bell` without breach | a **hostile act** by a party against the other (§6.4.1) → **betrayal** |
| `joint` | two citizens of the **same** nation | the nation's Call of `call_period` (offered after its adoption, before `follow_from + 3`) | each party is **present**: has a DEPART with `arrive_bell = S` whose opened destination (REVEAL, or the march's SettleTransit record) lies in the Call province; bounced or displaced arrivals count as present (reported separately) | a party not present → **absent** (a broken promise, not a betrayal) |

Lifecycle: `offered` (expires after 6 bells) → `active` on accept | `declined` | `withdrawn` → `kept` | `betrayed` | `absent` | `renounced` | `expired`; at season end an active nap is shown `open` ("unbroken (open)"). **Break** (`op 5`) ends the pact at `break_bell + 6`: a hostile act after the notice is `renounced`, before it a betrayal. Per citizen ≤ 6 active pacts. Non-binding: the record and the chronicle are the consequence. (`truce` is cut.)
