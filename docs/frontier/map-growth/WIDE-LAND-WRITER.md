### −2.2 The experimental identity proposal (`frontier-abi` `world_index::march`)

`HOST_IDENTITY_FULL_REF_V1 = 1` names the shape every wide stage must share; it is **defined, not carried**: `WideHolding::read` still refuses any version but the unallocated one, so no wide holding carries it, no host can be mustered and no march can be debited. It is experimental versioning, not a production protocol number.

* **Canonical identity**: the 56-byte `WideHostRef` (`world ‖ 17-byte sort key ‖ site ‖ gen ‖ seq`). Every ownership check compares it (`holding_ref()` for the issuing holding, as `holding_key_of_host`).
* **Roster alias** `wide_host_alias(ref) = 1<<63 | sha256("PSF-WIDE-HOST-ALIAS-v1" ‖ ref)[..8] >> 1`: the `u64` a wide province's entry table and the Holding-layout transit slot may carry for **lookup only**. `addr::split_host_id` refuses it (its province-index field is at least `2^19`), so no legacy reader parses it, and it is **never** an input of a kernel formula.
* **Wide formulas** `wide_slot_key(key, bell, ref)`, `wide_tie_key(seed, ref)`, `wide_clash_seed(seed, key, bell)` under the domains `frontier/slot/wide-v1`, `tie/wide-v1`, `frontier/clash/wide-v1`, over the world, the full sort key and the full reference. The legacy path keeps its formulas byte for byte. No handler uses the wide ones yet; they exist so that a wide reveal and resolve are written against one definition.
* **Transit record**: the 256-byte `WideTransit` at `with_seed(season, wm ‖ hash(holding ‖ slot))` (`transit_address`), not the 96-byte slot inside the Holding layout. The slot would keep the alias and the `in_transit` state so `host_in_transit` keeps refusing a second departure.

### −2.3 What the program now does (through the real `dispatch`)

WideDepart (0xDA) has the deployed Depart's shape: the player prologue, `[holding w] [province w] [system] [holding_province r]`, where `province` is the province the host **stands in** and `holding_province` the issuing holding's own (the earlier draft wrongly used the holding's province as the standing one). Checks, in the deployed order where it has one: the holding canonical and the Citizen's at the reference's generation; its own province present, canonical and not capture-locking it; the standing province a wide province of this world. **The boundary**: a deployed Province in the standing position is refused `NotImplemented` before anything else is read: the arrival quota, the roster caps and the resolution of a destination bell are one per province and bell, and a wide host standing in or marching across the disk would need the legacy and wide paths to share them, which they do not (separate quotas or rosters for one bell would skip combat or double garrison damage). The destination is sealed at Depart, so the boundary can only be judged where the host stands; a reveal would have to judge the sealed destination. Then: final, the standing province resident, the seal's syntax, the **identity gate** (`NotImplemented`: every wide holding is founded under the unallocated identity), the alias lookup in the standing roster (`NotResident`), the arrival window and end bell, the tip floor, the transit slot, the funds, and then an explicit final `NotImplemented`: with no settle, reveal, resolve or return to follow, a transit record and an escrow would be a debit into an unfinished destination. **Nothing is written or debited on any path.**

### −2.4a The clash kernel over the full identity (executable, same day)

`permutation-rules/src/frontier/clash.rs`: both bodies (`resolve_clash`, the Phase A production body, and `resolve_clash_ref`, the reference) are now one algorithm generic over `ClashIdentity`, the scheme that names hosts and garrisons, derives the clash seed from the province and the bell, gives each host its tie key, keys each engagement's variance dice and names the retreat wedge. The legacy scheme is `u64` (dense id, `i32` pair, `tie_key`, `clash_seed`, the 9-byte `city ‖ LE64(id)` key, `ProvinceCoord::wedge`, `sort_by_key3`), and the legacy entry points are its instantiation: every hash takes the same bytes and every sort the same total key, so legacy outcomes are unchanged (`frontier_clash_equiv.rs` keeps pinning 4,320 inputs; `frontier_clash_identity.rs` adds fixed data, input-order and insertion invariance, and a delegating newtype scheme that reproduces the legacy outcome field for field). `Fighter`, `Garrison`, `ClashInput`, `FighterResult`, `GarrisonResult` are aliases of the `…Of<u64>` generics; `ClashError` stays an enum (`From<ClashErrorOf<u64>>`); `ClashOutcome` is unchanged. `frontier-abi` `world_index::march`: `WideHostRef` implements the scheme (province = `ProvinceKey`, seed = `wide_clash_seed`, tie = `wide_tie_key`, key = `city ‖ 56-byte reference`, wedge = the coordinate's exact geometric `WideCoord::wedge`, stable sorts over the reference's order; the roster alias is never read). `resolve_wide_clash` runs the production body under it and `resolve_wide_clash_ref` the reference; tests show them equal on fixed data that fights, order- and insertion-invariant, results named by full references (the garrison by its holding's reference), duplicates refused by reference. `claim_alias` is the authenticated collision check a roster or transit writer must run before writing an alias (every table entry's alias must be its own reference's; the same alias under another reference is refused; the alias alone proves nothing). `wide_plain_commit` is the model of what a wide seal must commit to (host reference and full destination key inside the commitment); it is not a seal format: no ciphertext, beacon binding or bond exists. **This is the executable combat core of the wide world, not a movement**: no handler calls it, no wide roster, arrival record, ClashInputs or Reveal exists, and nothing is debited. It is an experimental proposal, not a decided production identity.

### −2.4b The wide seal, the arrival records, the roster side table (second review point, same day)

Built on the kernel milestone (`828128a7`). Still **no handler reads or writes any of it**; these are the versioned, authenticated components a wide Reveal, Gather, Resolve, SettleTransit and Muster must share, with fixed-byte tests and no key, signature or fixture transaction.

* **Wide seal** (`frontier-abi` `world_index::seal`): a 136-byte plaintext (version 2) that commits to the **full 56-byte host reference** and the **full 50-byte destination key**, the deployed path encoding, bell, tile, stance and retreat; sealed as `IBE (128, the deployed tlock block, unchanged) ‖ body (136)` = 264 bytes under the deployed keystream (`seal::keystream_xor`, the legacy 37-byte body's two blocks continued) and the commitment domain `PS-FRONTIER-MARCH-WIDE-v1`. The legacy `Plain37`, its 165-byte seal, its commitment and every vector are untouched. `validate` refuses a mismatched host, a synthetic identity, another arrival bell, a destination in another world, and the deployed path/tile/stance/retreat faults; a legacy-disk destination is a *valid* plaintext (the boundary is the reveal's to refuse by the standing roster, not the seal's). `permutation-rules` `seal` gained `keystream_xor` and `commit_slice`, both pinned equal to `body_xor`/`commit` over 37 bytes.
* **Opener** (`permutation-frontier` `crypto::seal`): `open` is now `recover_key` (the pairing, `sigma`, `k`, the FO check: the same steps in the same order over the 128-byte IBE block) followed by the 37-byte body; `open_wide`/`judge_wide` (feature-gated) run the same `recover_key` and then the wide body and `world_index::seal::validate`. **Untested cryptography**: the pairing and FO check are the deployed code and the host cannot run them; the wide tests reuse the recorded seal vectors' IBE blocks and keys and pin only the body/commitment path (a wide plaintext sealed under a vector's `k` behind the vector's IBE block opens to itself under the wide commitment and to nothing under the legacy commitment or a wrong key).
* **Arrival records** (`world_index::arrival`): wide ArrivalSlot (288 B) and ClashInputs (3,008 B, 24 × 112-byte positions) keyed by the full destination key, arrivals named by full reference and Citizen key, the deployed quota, flags and fate codes, a conversion to the kernel's `WideFighter`; with-seed addresses under provisional tags `wa`/`wi`. Every read and write checks the host is of the destination's **world** (an arrival's reference keeps its home province, which normally differs from the destination), every typed field (faction, slot, unit, stance, tile, retreat ≤ 60,000, flags, fate) and, for ClashInputs, the header (magic, version, reserved bytes, a decodable key, masks and `n_present` in range) before any position. The extra rent over the deployed 160/1,280 bytes is an explicit experimental storage cost; no deployed fee changes.
* **Roster side table** (`world_index::roster`): one full reference per Province entry index (3,248 B, tag `wo`, immutable original rent recipient), its header naming the province the hosts **stand in**. Every method takes the roster it expects and refuses bytes whose header is another's (province or season); `claim` is the only write (same world required, any home province; `march::claim_alias` over the whole table; returns the alias the entry carries), `entry_matches` the only trusted read of one entry. `table` **regenerates** aliases from the stored references and proves nothing about the Province's actual `u64` entries; `validate_paired` is the explicit check of the two tables against each other a handler must run first.
* **Tagged combat identity** (`march::WideCombatant`): the kernel's wide identity is `Host(WideHostRef) | Camp { key, gen: u32 } | Keep { key, gen: u32 }`, 57 bytes under a tag byte. The host codec stays strict (no site ≥ 12 decodes anywhere), the camp and keep carry the full `u32` generation the deployed records keep, no variant decodes as another, and `resolve_wide_clash` refuses a synthetic resident or arrival before the kernel runs; a garrison may be any variant. The roster, the arrival records and the seal store hosts only, so none of them can hold a camp or keep.
* **Keystream bound** (`seal::keystream_xor`): at most 8,192 bytes (the one-byte block counter), a longer body refused untouched; pinned at 8,192 and 8,193.
* **The beacon binds the bell, not the region.** All regions of one bell share the round `T(arrive)`; a seal that opens proves the bell, not the destination's region. A wide reveal or settle must take the destination from the committed plaintext, derive its region by the configured experimental rule, require THE anchor or archive entry of that region and bell at its canonical address, open with `T(arrive)` and take the combat seed `S(A)` from that anchor. The host BLS syscalls are fail-closed stubs: **no test demonstrates a real decryption of a wide seal**; `open_body` under a recorded key is a body/commitment check only.

Blocked, unchanged: no wide Muster, Depart effects, SettleDeparture, Reveal (the walk over wide provinces, the quota by `wide_slot_key`, the latch), Gather, Resolve, SettleTransit (seal judgement against the region's anchor, refunds, forfeit, strand, return), return settle; no keeper, verifier or herald support; no wide sealer (the off-chain tlock encryption of a 136-byte body is `fclient`'s to add, over the same IBE block). Cross-boundary moves stay refused at Depart.

## −1. Third slice (2026-11-07, on `87088006`, same worktree): the wide outpost ticket through the deployed lottery

Written with source tools only; not compiled or run by its author (the native worker runs §−1.6). Host-only as before. It supersedes §0's rows for 0xD9 and the "who may act" paragraph.

**Escrow retention correction (2026-11-07, same uncommitted slice, after native validation of the first draft).** The first draft returned the record whole to its funder at every ticket end. That is economically different from the deployed escrow, which stays in the Citizen after an expired or taken ticket until a refile reuses it or CloseCitizen returns it. The correction below reproduces the deployed `FileOutpost` / `FileTicket` escrow top-up (`need − escrow`, the payer becomes `TICKET_FUNDER` only when it tops up, `escrow = max(escrow, need)`), the deployed `SettleTicket` consumption (exactly one rent at a win, nothing when expired or taken) and the deployed `CloseCitizen` refund (whole to the funder, at season end only) over the isolated record: an explicit `Settled` record status, an explicit `ESCROW` field, no refund while the Season runs, and a season-end close that works before or after the Citizen's own close. The settle's account list lost its funder position (10/13 accounts, as the deployed SettleTicket names no funder). Not compiled or run by its author.

**Second retention review, same day.** With a single mutable funder, a payer B who only topped up the escrow for a refile would have received A's record rent at the close. The record now keeps the deployed Citizen's two recipients apart: an **immutable `RENT_PAYER`** (who created the record, as the Citizen's `RENT_PAYER`) beside the mutable `FUNDER` (the Citizen's `TICKET_FUNDER`). WideClose(0) is CloseCitizen's shape: `[... ] [rent_payer w] [funder w]?`, the funder listed exactly when the escrow is non-zero (`TooManyAccounts` otherwise); the escrow moves to the funder, then the record closes **whole** to the rent payer (its rent and any excess lamports, the deployed remainder policy; a whole-account refund never diverts). Both recipients are checked against the record's stored keys, so neither a Citizen close, an NFT transfer nor a later payer can redirect them. The codec changed identity: magic `PSFXWTK2`, 192 bytes; the first draft's `PSFXWTK1` (160 bytes, never committed) is refused by magic and by length, so no reserved byte is ever read as an escrow.

### −1.2 The marker, the escrow and the deployed readers

The Citizen's deployed ticket fields hold the marker while a wide ticket is pending: `TICKET_BELL` = the filing bell, `TICKET_SITES` all zero (no deployed ticket has zero sites), `TICKET_NEXT` 0, the `slots` ticket bits = the slot; the capture reservation bits, the shared `TICKET_ESCROW`, `TICKET_FUNDER` and the NFT resident fields are untouched. Readers that see it: the program paths above; the keeper's ticket settler, which reads zero sites and skips the Citizen (`land::ticket_sites` → no site at `ticket_next`); the herald's and the web client's Citizen views, which show an open ticket with no sites; the verifier, which cannot replay a draft tag and rejects the draft tags as UnknownInstruction/FAIL at V0 (nothing here extends its known tags or its checks; these are unsupported experimental instructions, not evidence of user misconduct). The wide Holding's rent is **never** in `TICKET_ESCROW`: that pool is sized at the deployed 1,280-byte rent and shared with capture reservations; the record holds the wide rent instead, in its own `ESCROW` field with its own `FUNDER` (the deployed `TICKET_FUNDER`) beside an immutable `RENT_PAYER` (the deployed Citizen `RENT_PAYER`: whoever created the record), and keeps it between tickets exactly as the Citizen keeps the deployed escrow. After a settle the Citizen carries no marker, so every deployed path (SettleTicket `NoTicket`, CloseCitizen, DeclareSiege, the keeper's settler, the readers) sees a Citizen with no ticket; the retained wide rent is visible only in the record. CloseCitizen can therefore close a Citizen beside a Settled record; the record is not orphaned: WideClose(0) closes it (escrow to the funder, the rest to the rent payer) with the absent Citizen in its position, and its canonical address and stored citizen key tie it to that Citizen.

### −1.3 Preserved and still open

Preserved as deployed: the settler cost is burned at the filing and never refunded; no cancellation exists (a ticket ends by settle: expired, taken, won, or by the season-end close); the lottery ordering (fresh/displace/taken, the tie to the lower tag), the cohort once-only handling, the 24-bell expiry, the finality condition (timestamp **and** cohort closure), the first-home flags, the three slots and the one inventory; **the escrow's life**: topped up to one rent at a filing with the funder re-attributed only by a top-up, one rent consumed by a win, retained after expired/taken, reused by the next filing, returned at season end only, the escrow to the funder and the account's rent and remainder to the immutable rent payer (CloseCitizen's two recipients). The one structural difference that remains is *where* the escrow lives (the record, not the Citizen) and *which* rent it is (1,344 B, not 1,280 B); the deployed `escrow_need` multiplier for capture reservations does not apply to it because a capture reservation is refused while a wide ticket is pending and the shared escrow never counts the wide rent.

Known remaining differences and limits of this correction: the deployed CloseCitizen returns the escrow and closes the Citizen in **one** instruction; here a Settled record needs its own WideClose(0) (before or after CloseCitizen), so a season-end sweep must list the records too (anyone may send it; the recipients are the record's), and an unclosed record stays at its address like any unclosed deployed account. The host harness cannot shrink an account in place, so the tombstone test uses a second account at the Season's key (a fixture).

Open: no productive wide-holding action exists (Harvest, Build, Train, Explore, Muster do not accept a wide holding), so a wide holding is owned land that produces nothing yet; WideDepart stays refused at the host-identity gate; no PS2 record kind for a wide filing, settlement or opening (the anchor's `HARVEST` and a displacement's `DIVERT` are the only records); the keeper, herald, web and verifier have no wide ticket or wide holding support; the candidate rules of §0 are unchanged and unapproved.

### −1.4 Atomicity in the host harness

The runtime reverts a refused transaction; the host harness has no rollback. The handlers preflight the named deterministic transitions before writes, without claiming that every possible error is pre-write: each remaining transition (the bucket, the marker, the cohort, the slot's clear-and-bind, the displaced slot's unbind, the counters) runs first on a scratch copy, and the one lamport-moving step runs before every byte write. The deployed `init_with_seed` tops the record up before it allocates, so a refused allocation at a filing leaves a top-up the chain reverts. The wide tests run every wide instruction through a snapshot-and-restore executor (`run_tx`: lamports, bytes, length, owner of every account) and report whether a refusal had written anything; the "nothing changed" assertions are evidence under that emulation, not of the chain.

What is **not** blocked and was not built on purpose: a payer-funded terrain-only land account (refused as not the game), a side inventory of wide holdings (refused: it would be a fourth slot), a march that debits without a settle (refused by `WideHolding::read` and `WideTransit::depart`), a wide `OpenRing` on the legacy Frontier (the wide seed chain is self-contained so legacy readers never see a ring past 128), and any change to `R_MAX_HARD`, `host_id`, the deployed layouts, the verifier or the history checks.

## 4. Safety properties the tests pin

- **No alias.** Wide addresses use a separate domain from legacy Province, RingSeed and Holding addresses (the legacy grammar is untouched); tested samples do not alias, but this is not a proof against cryptographic collisions. No wide record passes a legacy presence check: different magic, different size, `layout_version 3`.
- **No relabel.** Prefix `P`, `Q`, `RING` of a wide province are zero and a nonzero value is refused (`LegacyIdentityInPrefix`); the dense index and `host_id` are never computed for a wide coordinate.
- **No genesis substitute.** A wide ring seed has one path: request (round fixed) → verified consume. A zero seed is refused as a seed; a requested record with a seed is refused as malformed.
- **One inventory.** A slot is spent in exactly one representation; the tested legacy slot allocator skips a wide-bound slot; complete legacy-reader and writer compatibility remains unproven; a wide binding counts in `holdings_n`; slot 1 stays legacy.
- **No partial debit.** A wide holding with any issued host, transit or explore under identity version 0 is unreadable; a wide departure is refused at the identity gate before any escrow.
- **Fail closed, write nothing.** Every refusing handler is compared before and after on the fund, payer, Citizen, holding and seed count.
